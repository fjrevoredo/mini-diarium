Retention measured in unlock events rather than days is what let users investigating the 0.6.4 data-loss regression overwrite their own pre-incident history; the assessment in [`docs/reports/2026-08-04-backup-system-assessment.md`](../reports/2026-08-04-backup-system-assessment.md) records 17 findings (5 High) and recommends Option A. Requirements: retention guarantees time depth independently of how often the journal is opened; snapshots are taken before risky writes (pending schema migrations, `reset_diary`, journal moves, auth-method removal, import) rather than after them; snapshots are written atomically and verified before being counted; an unchanged journal produces no new snapshot; the user can see backup state and health from the app, including from the unlock screen; and both restore granularities work in-app with no plaintext ever written to disk — whole-journal rollback (taking a safety snapshot first) and per-entry restore from inside a snapshot. Constraints: snapshots stay ordinary encrypted Mini Diarium databases (no new container format, no new cryptography); retention policy stays pure and filesystem access stays behind a narrow boundary so the open-core split and any future tier are not foreclosed; no network in the core. Entry-level revision history is explicitly **out of scope** here and tracked separately as the follow-up option. Acceptance: the five High findings (B-1 to B-5) are closed with tests, `website/docs-src/09-backups.md` is rewritten and the five other stale documentation claims corrected, and a restore is demonstrated end-to-end from a snapshot the app produced itself. Staged delivery, the resolved design decisions, and the UX gate live in [`docs/backup-system-redesign-plan.md`](../backup-system-redesign-plan.md).
  - **Progress (2026-08-04): Milestones 1–2 complete** — snapshot engine (`VACUUM INTO` + fsync + atomic rename + verification, tiered retention, change-counter dedup, storage budget, pre-migration/destructive/lock/exit triggers, manifest sidecar, legacy adoption, background threading, `backup_bench`) and the documentation corrections shipped.
